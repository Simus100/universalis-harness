A single continuous macro shot of deep indigo ink dispersing in still water, lit from behind. The ink
is being pulled by a slow current: the swirling plumes stretch and align into thin parallel streaks
all moving in the same direction, like a river seen from very close, while the water stays black.
Behind the ink, in the upper right, a soft cold edge of light begins to glow through the cloud: a
blade of light forming, still soft, still shapeless, not yet a line. The lower centre of the frame
stays a clean dark negative space. Extremely shallow depth of field, large oval bokeh in the
background. Camera: 100mm macro lens, f/2.8, slow continuous push-in with a gentle 2-degree tilt up,
as if following the current, no shake, no handheld. Light: one hard cold backlight from the upper
right, everything else in darkness. Look: shot on 35mm film, fine natural grain, halation around the
brightest edges, deep teal-blue shadows, violet midtones, no warm colours, no orange, low saturation,
high dynamic range. Motion is slow and continuous, like 120fps slowed to 24fps. The ink must stay
ink: no tunnel, no grid, no architecture, no geometric structure. No text, no letters, no words, no
people, no objects, no logos, no watermark, no cuts, one single continuous take, 8 seconds.
