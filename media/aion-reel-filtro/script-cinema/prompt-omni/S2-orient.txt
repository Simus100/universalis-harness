A 6-second single continuous macro shot of deep indigo ink dispersing in still water, lit from behind.
A slow current pulls the ink: the swirling plumes stretch and align into thin parallel streaks all
moving in the same direction, like a river seen from very close, while the water stays black. The
frame contains only water, ink and light. Behind the ink, in the upper right, a soft cold edge of
light begins to glow through the cloud: the beginning of a blade of light, still soft, still
shapeless, never becoming a line in this shot. The lower centre of the frame stays a clean dark area
with nothing in it. Extremely shallow depth of field, large oval bokeh in the background. Camera:
100mm macro lens, f/2.8, slow continuous push-in with a gentle 2-degree tilt up, as if following the
current, smooth and steady. Light: one hard cold backlight from the upper right, darkness everywhere
else. Look: shot on 35mm film, fine natural grain, halation around the brightest edges, deep teal-blue
shadows, violet midtones, low saturation, high dynamic range. The palette is blue, violet and black
only. The ink stays ink: it never becomes a tunnel, a corridor, a grid or architecture. Audio: no
music, no speech. Only a wide low filtered room tone, with the sub under it slowly pulsing about twice
a second. One single continuous take, no cuts, no on-screen text.
