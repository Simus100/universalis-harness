A 6-second single continuous macro shot of deep indigo ink dispersing in still water, lit from behind.
Slow, viscous plumes of ink open and fold in the dark water, suspended, as if the water were thick.
The frame contains only three things: black water, indigo ink, and one cold backlight coming from the
upper right. The material is liquid ink in water, never smoke and never floating particles. Most of
the frame is near-black empty water; the ink occupies the upper two thirds and the edges, and the
lower centre of the frame stays a clean dark area with nothing in it. Extremely shallow depth of
field: a few pockets of ink are razor sharp, everything else melts into large oval bokeh. Camera:
100mm macro lens, f/2.8, one slow continuous push-in of a few centimetres across the whole shot.
Camera stays smooth and steady. Light: a single hard cold backlight that rim-lights the ink, and
total darkness everywhere else. Look: shot on 35mm film, fine natural grain, halation around the
brightest ink edges, deep teal-blue shadows, violet midtones, low saturation, high dynamic range.
There is no warmth in the frame: the palette is blue, violet and black only. Motion is heavy and
suspended, like 120fps slowed to 24fps. Audio: no music, no speech, no voices. Only a wide, low,
filtered room tone with a faint 40 Hz sub underneath it, like the sound of water heard through a
wall. One single continuous take, no cuts, no on-screen text.
